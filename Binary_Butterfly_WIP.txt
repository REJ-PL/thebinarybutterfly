Prologue
Within one of the many thousands of similar apartments, a hodgepodge of mismatched furniture and dim lighting, seemed to hold its breath, waiting for its occupant; a tall woman with bronze hair attired in a slim tee and some cotton slacks. The sun's relentless assault through the window added to her discomfort, creating shimmering patches of brilliance in the dusty room. The city outside, bathed in the early morning glow, showed signs of stirring to life.
She leaned against the cluttered desk, her fingers tracing the outlines of old scratches etched into its surface. The room was stifling, the air conditioner's hum a feeble attempt to combat the oppressive July heat. Beads of sweat clung to her brow, glistening like liquid diamonds in the sultry air.
The line crackled briefly, the faint buzz of static filling Mellie's consciousness, amplifying the atmosphere of anticipation that hung in the warm morning air. She thumbed at a smudge on the glass as she stared through, out into the city.
As she held her breath, waiting for a response, sweat trickled down her temple. Simple human evidence of the faulty air conditioning unit that struggled to combat the rising heat as July arrived; her cramped apartment becoming an insufferable claustrophobic oven. The thick humidity only heightened the tension, adding a physically uncomfortable layer to the already charged moment.
"Sure, okay..." an accented voice grumbled from the speaker carrying a hint of weariness. "You bring Garro, I can set you up with a decent decker. The job's yours. Meet me at my place tomorrow."
The line abruptly cut out, leaving Mellie alone with her thoughts. The words echoed in her mind: The job’s yours; fuelling a surge of adrenaline. This was the break she had been waiting for, an opportunity to dive into the shadows of Akureyri City, the place she called home. A chance to fight back.
The promise of a skilled decker and the allure of the job sent waves of excitement coursing through her veins, like a cool breeze cutting through the cloying heat.
A mischievous smile crept across her face, the corner of her mouth twitching upward into a smirk. "About time we saw some action," she reflexively addressed a photo hung on her wall. Her voice was laced with a mix of excitement and confidence. To her, this job sounded like easy cash and a launch pad for what she needed to do next.
 
On the other side of the Skrim—a towering expanse of glass and steel; an area that appeared as if all that had been discarded from other parts of the city had inadvertently congealed into a distinct district within the urban landscape—a wiry and austere figure perched in a chair far too grand for his lean frame.
A sneer tugged at the corners of his lips as he casually spat out derogatory words under his breath, but aside from the words he spoke, he showed an air of lucid satisfaction.
"Dumb bitch," he hissed, his voice touched with apparent disdain. With a dismissive flick, he swiped away the holographic display that had been showcasing snippets of Mellie's personal information. His contempt for her was evident, but this was a man for whom contempt was the default, very select few earned his true friendship - he didn’t have time or the need for ‘friends’.
Activating his embedded agent, a small implanted device connected to his synthetic eye; the man delved into its vast capabilities. The agent served as his internal computer, telephone, and information repository, connecting him to a network of equally unsavoury individuals.
Scrolling past a dozen shady-looking faces, his gaze settled on one particular profile. This was a person who seemed to possess the skills he required. With a swift gesture, he initiated another call, his agent bleeping a confirmation of his command.
The city's distant ambient electronic interference muddled with the faint electronic beeping resonating from a speaker embedded in the desk before him, an auditory reminder of the twisted web of connections that permeated throughout Akureyri City.
As the transmission connected, the screen in Davie's synthetic eye merged reality with video, revealing the face of the person he sought to engage. A rugged visage stared back at him, a face etched with lines of experience and the weariness of a life spent at the edge of society.
"Gerki!" Davie called out, his voice muffling in the empty room. "Gerki, I need you on a job. It's a simple thing. Just be the backstop for a couple of nobodies. I can't afford to let this one fall through." His words held a sense of urgency, a hint of desperation that he tried to conceal.
Davie slowly swivelled his chair, allowing himself to gaze at a gaudy, near-pornographic painting adorning the side of his office. His idle observation and the rancid thoughts it sparked in him were a stark display of the decadence and debauchery that hides all that lurks in this city, a city fuelled by excess and indulgence, riding on the broken backs of desire and hope.
"Yeah, I swear, this gig is totally off any Corpo radar," Davie continued, his voice oily with a mix of excitement and reassurance. "It's practically an invisible job. So, my place, tomorrow, okay?"
His fingers danced along the edge of his desk, tracing the intricate patterns carved into the fine woodwork. His gaze fixed on the cigar resting on the desk, his hand gave up flicking at a rough notch in the wood and reached out to claim it. Tapping the cigar against the woodwork, he contemplated the smoke-filled possibilities that lay ahead; all the while half listening to a conversation only he and his unseen partner of communique were privy to.
In response to his internalized discussion with his digitized companion, he shifted - lifted by a newfound sense of enthusiasm. A thin smile curled from his lips.
Davie's voice rose once more, the stifled enthusiasm laced with a subtle tone of appeasement. "Great! I'll see you tomorrow then!" His thick accent betrayed his origins, hinting at a past that lay far from where he now sat.
Davie picked up the cigar, clipped the end, lit it, and sucked in the first draw of fire and smoke. He swung back in his chair.
Best fucking gig ever, he thought to himself as he took a long, self-satisfied drag of the thick tobacco. The bitter, woody, floral aroma filled the room, mingling with the ever-present human stench of the Skrim.
In the depths of the cyberpunk metropolis, where shadows danced with deceit and hope flickered like distant stars, they would all discover that the price of success was steep, and the cost of failure, cataclysmic.
Of course, at that time, Davie didn’t know.
Nobody knew; 
The end of the world had just begun.
Chapter One:	The Neon Pulse of Akureyri City
Akureyri City doesn't just exist; it breathes. A living organism of concrete and neon, pulsating with the dreams and despair of its inhabitants. Nearly eight million lives ticking in abstracted unison.
A microcosm self-contained world where cultures clash and political ideologies battle from the streets to the towers, where the senses are awakened and numbed with equal efficacy, and the soul is stirred or laid bare at the ephemeral whim of the city's essence.
In the alleys and plazas where the scent of street food mingles with exhaust fumes, families bustle through the urban sprawl. Children laugh in happy youthful ignorance, darting between housing complexes and playing amid the worn-down or vacant commercial outlets. Those void-filled storefronts and graffiti-marked walls become their playground, a city of forgotten places.
The hum of asphalt arteries forming the transport routes that carry the tide and flow of people, all needing to be everywhere else, all at once - vibrates in the background. Amongst the densest areas. A constant drone of ceaseless motion. And, further out, the rising pulse and surge of commute and life. 
It is a place where industrial odours assault your nostrils. The monotonous gray of high-rise apartments is broken by the sudden flash of a holographic advertisement, selling dreams to the weary residents. Rainwater mixes with oil, painting the streets with iridescent despair.
For most, existence is a nightmare. Litter blows along garbage-strewn alleys where the masses trudge past; eyes downcast. The stench of waste and body odour fills the air, mingling with clouds of choking fumes billowing from pipes and vents. Flickering neon signs cast an eerie glow on broken pavements smeared with grime. Around crumbling corners, mismatched buildings stacked tight block out the sky. The only hints of nature are ragged weeds pushing up through cracks. 
Above those streets, gleaming like jewels reaching to the heavens; extravagance forever out of reach but sharing that same space. Displaced by chance or fortune, are those few who, by the stepping-stone of sufferance, find themselves living in glinting glass towers that taunt their perfunctory neighbours.
Ambivalent to those far below, the elite peer through floor-to-ceiling windows where the sprawling city is reduced to neat grids, squalor obscured by distance. Silk and leather rustle. Diamonds and gold glitter under dazzling lights. 
Every surface gleams, from onyx floors to marble walls, a gaudy display of wealth. In plush lounges scented with rare flowers, the elite laugh and chatter while sipping extravagant cocktails, their voices echoing with the clink of ice in fine crystal. Every glance is a negotiation; every touch is a contract.
In the city's heart, its core; power. The cold gaze of security cameras and the silent hum of monolithic buildings. The gleam of darkened or mirrored windows reflects the faces of those who are untouched by the problems outside of those panes, distorted and multiplied, an unseen gluttony of ambition and control.
Money whispers in the wind, a siren's call, a trap, one that seems to linger ponderously; falling from those heights and leaking to the districts below. The taste of overripe fruit from street vendors lingers; a sweet decay that mirrors the environment. Unexpected laughter erupts from a shadowed alley, a jarring note in a symphony of excess and neglect.
Districts marked by the spray of graffiti. The tang of fresh paint mingles with the sound of distant sirens. Gang symbols leer from the walls, watching every move. The sudden roar of powerful tuned engines intersperses the background hum, only to fade to the sounds of the distant clang of machinery, the metal fatigue of creaking giant container vessels in the docks, and the murmur of secret deals sealed in dark corners. 
The glow of neon on wet pavement paints an eerie portrait, a dance of shadows and secrets. A cat's yowl cuts through the night, a primal cry in a world of technology.
Just as a cat would, you could lose yourself in the chaos of the sprawl. The sizzle of street food, the clang of industry; and the babble of commerce create an overwhelming sensory buffet. A sudden downpour sends people scurrying, their reflections shimmering in puddles, a fleeting glimpse of life's impermanence.
Through rain-slicked alleys and neon-lit streets, Akureyri City pulses, a living entity that draws you into its embrace.
As the city's holographic billboards flash with advertisements for the latest implant enhancements and luxury products, a deeper game was being played. A game where the stakes were life and death, where alliances were forged and broken, and where the lines between right and wrong were blurred by the neon haze.
The story of Akureyri City is written a hundred times over in each new day, and in those stories is the one that will become a tale that will become a legend. The pieces were moving into place. The board was set, the players were ready, and the game was about to begin.
In the end, the people in those stories would all learn that in Akureyri City, survival was not a right but a privilege; and that every choice, every action, had a price. The line between hero and villain was thin.
The final story of Akureyri City was about to be written, and it all began with a single phone call.
Chapter Two:	A Ripple in Code.
The early signs of winter were creeping into Akureyri City much sooner than usual. The city wasn't normally this cold, the kind of cold that infused deep into the bones, leaving a lingering discomfort. Zecsa scowled, scrunching her face in frustration as she kicked the malfunctioning heater once more as if to defy the elements themselves. "Fucking thing!" she exclaimed; her voice laced with annoyance that the empty apartment swallowed.
The closed apartment door seemed to mock her, an impassive barrier against her internalised aggravation. Zecsa wrinkled her nose as though to snub the indigency of the door. She mentally ticked a box, she had already made a choice; a flicker of begrudgement crossed her face as she turned away, determined not to let the unwelcome coldness consume her spirit.
In her newfound pursuit of warmth, Zecsa marched towards her closet, discarding a series of inadequate options with erratic disdain. Finally, her hands found solace in the shape of a fairly thick summer jacket. It lacked the necessary insulation but was a welcome shield against the bracing chill. Clawing her way into the denim, she wrapped herself tightly, seeking refuge in its feeble embrace.
She stepped back into the main room; her eyes were instinctively drawn to the commanding presence of the sizable computer rig that she had. Within the confines of this digital sanctuary, she found refuge from the relentless grip of physical discomfort. The soft hum of the machine whispered promises of extrication.
The glow of the monitors bathed the room in an unnatural light, birthing lethargic icy blue shadows that rhythmically undulated across the walls. Although alone, it was here that she felt acceptance.
Amidst the crisp surroundings, the most inviting presence in the room emanated from a cup of freshly brewed coffee. Zecsa eagerly grasped it with both hands, pulling it close to her lips. The rising steam brushed against her cool face, offering a brief reprieve from the unwelcome atmosphere. She savoured the gentle warmth that caressed her, allowing it to brush the prickling numbness that clung to her cheeks, if only for a fleeting moment.
Setting the cup aside, Zecsa's attention turned to the scrolling text outputs on one of the screens. The sea of information unfolded before her, a torrent of data and possibilities. Her keen eyes scan the lines, a hunter searching for elusive prey. Words jump out, vying for her attention, each one a breadcrumb leading her closer to a binary-encoded trophy. She pauses, her mind contemplating the implications of a particular section of code before shaking her head, dismissing it back into the constellations of the digital universe from which it emerged.
Her world is code, the stuff of life. In the code, you could find things, important things, valuable things, and that is what she does best. Within the unending symphony of digital pulses that held the city together, Zecsa could discover treasure. In the past weeks, her tireless pursuit had yielded a bountiful haul, earning her a handsome sum of five thousand dollies from a piece of coveted corpo marketing data.
She knew the worth of every byte, every line, and her mantra remained emblazoned at the back of her mind, "It's all valuable, ya just have to find the right buyer."
That aspect of the job was one Zecsa preferred to keep at arm's length. Past encounters had ratified cautionary reminders of the darker side of her trade, those who sought such valuable information were rarely amiable fellows. Yet, Zecsa had crafted a personal directory, a hidden trove of trusted contacts reserved for when business called. So, while she avoided direct involvement in such transactions, her little black book held the key to navigating the shadows with calculated finesse.
And despite her landlord's attempts to freeze her to the bone, Zecsa knew the importance of making rent. "Can't run a rig like this on the street," she asserted inwardly, recognizing the necessity of a stable haven for her digital pursuits. As she took a sip of the coffee, basking in its warm aura, she directed her attention back to the text before her. Her focus, submerged in the scrolling lines, her mind attuned to detecting the slightest anomaly.
And there it was—a glimmer of peculiarity that beckons her curiosity.
Her head tilts, her eyes narrow, a pixelated allurement, something unexpected.
In a swift, instinctive motion, her fingers move in perfect synchrony with her internal cerebral net-link, she rewinds the feed, freezing and seizing the crucial moment, extracting the text that had captivated her attention.
"Bulletin: Urgent net information packet delivery required – d=D 500,000."
She re-reads the message, her disbelief softly verbalised into the room. "No fucking way..." she exclaimed, her voice dropping to a low, almost incredulous tone
 A sense of urgency grips her, and with an unconscious reaction, she begins tracing the net-code route of the output. 
The weight of sudden realization bore down on her, and with a surge of panic, she swiftly isolates the information, cutting it off from prying eyes. At that same moment, injecting random noise into the feed to mask where the data once was. Even if this turned out to be a ruse, she wouldn't take any chances. No reason to let anyone else see it.
Leaning back in her chair, Zecsa takes another sip of her coffee, desperately trying to ground herself.
"Nahh, this can't be for real... fuckin'... nah," she muttered to herself, trying to dismiss the enormity of the potential of her discovery. But the draw of the coded message proves too strong to resist.
She re-reads the lines, her eyes dilating as her mind delves deeper into its hidden potential. To one side, a screen now blinking; the origin is traced back to a corporation, not just any corporation, but M.A.F—Meryil, Asukaga, and Fakhir.
The weight of their influence towers over Akureyri, shrouding the very heart of the city. A financial giant with ties to international governments and the military. The short luminous message prompts both a magnetic simplistic desire and a damning klaxon of portent.
Zecsa, sucks in a deep breath, inhaling the bitter scent of her coffee mingled with the gelid dampness of her apartment. The realization hit her with such force that she couldn't help but let out a single long, low, over-emphasized word; "Fuck."
The sheer magnitude of those few simple words of text lazily glowing from her screen sent her mind racing. If she sends a return confirmation, if she made her move, what would it mean?
The connection between her and the powerful corporate forces that governed Akureyri would become undeniably intertwined. Deep down, a lurking intuition whispered to her that this moment was inevitable. In a world in which the shadows offer comfort, she suddenly felt a spotlight sensation of vulnerability. 
It was a moment of clarity, of profound understanding. In the dimly lit room, the faint teal haze emitted by the screens cast Zecsa as an apparition, her silhouette shimmering in the gloom.
Determination ignites in Zecsa's eyes. The sharpness of the world seems to fade away, leaving only the weight of the decision pressing upon her. This was a crossroads, she felt it; a pivotal moment where her path diverges. She had already made her choice.

Zecsa's weary body protested against the strain of the night's endeavours, each dry cough serving as a reminder of the relentless test of her stamina.
Despite her fatigue, she couldn't tear her gaze away from the mesmerizing lines of code. The more she pulled apart the threads of its intricacies, the more tangible and unsettling it became. The realization sent electricity through her veins.
Yet, as wonderous as the mysterious message was, there had arisen a cause for concern that tugged at her.
Despite her meticulous efforts to isolate the message, she had failed to eliminate all traces of its presence. A sense of unease rested on her throughout the night as she came to realise that someone else had managed to connect to the same root net-code she had explored. The implications were grave: it meant that this unknown entity had also witnessed the original message.
Zecsa had dedicated a significant portion of the night playing detective, following her virtual footsteps, retracing her cybernated trails. Desperately seeking to pick out the invisible breadcrumbs of the unseen guest.
The layers of encryption she had woven around her; a protective shield, now felt fragile and insufficient but she had to believe whoever it was hadn't been able to find her, just as they also remained an enigma.
Her attempts to parry this unseen adversary's manoeuvres were no more fruitful than swatting a swarm of mosquitoes, you may shoo them away, but you still get bitten. Each countermove she made seemed to be deftly sidestepped — the intruder, clearly someone capable, had deployed a series of rotating virtual connections, a veritable maze of shifting sliding secret passageways, shrouding their true identity, leaving Zecsa feeling uncomfortably on edge.
The world outside her cluttered sanctuary seemed distant and inconsequential, overshadowed by the weight of her discoveries.
The cups on her desk, once serving as fuel for her determination, now appeared as mere artefacts of a forgotten struggle.
Her focus narrowed, honing in on the task at hand, as she mentally mapped out the next steps. This was not a time for hesitation or doubt. Zecsa knew she had to seize this opportunity, disregarding the forces that lurked beyond the gossamer glow of her screens.
The sounds of early morning traffic accompanied by an emergent breeze both teased through the imperfectly sealed window. But, the breeze, the mess, and even the unknown visitors in the net held no significance. Zecsa had gathered a choice selection of seemingly important details, and her thoughts were consumed entirely, wrestling with them in priority.
Absently scratching her cheek, she stared at the screens displaying a quagmire of information, her eyes darting between them.
"Come on, girl, you can figure this out," she muttered to herself, determination etched across her face. "So, it's not in the net. But it's still just data. It's gotta be somewhere," she mused, pulling the corners of her jacket's collar tight.
"Okay, so, all I need to do is get the data, just like downloading it," she reasoned, her eyebrows furrowing in disagreement with the screen's lack of answers. "But I can't just fuckin' download what I can't access."
Restless, she rose from her chair and paced back toward the hot water tap, mechanically reaching for another empty mug and filling it. Unconsciously, she mixes in some Ready-Caf, her actions automatic, passive effects of her over-tired mind.
Continuing her agitated pacing, Zecsa points an accusing finger at the largest screen, her voice tinged with a mix of frustration and determination. "Fine, so I need to download it by actually getting a hard copy," she declared, her tone, defiant.
In a mimicry of her own voice, but deepened, she responds to her doubts; "Oh yeah, you're just gonna walk into some corpo plaza and just take some of their shit." Pausing to stare at the screen once more.
"Nah, not me," she murmurs, her eyes squinting, heavy with exhaustion and uncertainty.
"But... It's enough cash. I could hire someone to do it." The verbalised thought flitters through the air, agreed upon by nobody.
She looks away from the screen, allowing the thought to roll through her brain. Then, her gaze lands on the cup she's holding, a tinge of confusion crossing her face as if she's just realized the existence of cups for the first time. 
Acknowledging reality, she takes a sip.
With a slight shake of her head to bring her thoughts back, she continues aloud, "They can have a cut once I make the delivery. And I'll still earn enough dolly to be sitting pretty for a while." Her mind swirling with possibilities, each carrying risks.
Seeking validation for her audacious plan, she turns again to the screens, as if willing them to respond, to offer guidance from their unchanging displays of code and sections of data. Her words hang in the air, an unspoken plea for clarity and direction.
The room fills with an expectant silence, awaiting her final decision. Zecsa stood there, caught between the allure of a substantial financial gain, the unidentified digital gate-crasher, and the potential consequences of her actions of poking the sleeping bear that is M.A.F. The apathetic screen stared back at her.
"Mmm," Zecsa's lips formed a subtle pout, an indication that her brain had already fallen six layers deep into a mental haystack of calculations. Setting the coffee among its depleted companions, she settled back into her seat, fingers drumming lightly on the desk's surface. Quizzically glancing at her hand, and the desk, it was as if her agile digits held the secrets to unlocking the questions before her but until now had failed to impart their wisdom.
With a swift motion, she flips open a cover, revealing an in-set microphone and bringing to life yet another tiny screen. Her fingers dance across the screen, scrolling through a digital tapestry of names, each one a potential thread in the fabric of her emerging plan. Allowing her fingers to glide across the interface, scrolling through a seemingly endless list of names, until they find their target. A gentle touch, and the adjacent speaker crackles to life, the sound of electronics shaking off the remnants of the night's slumber, followed by a trill of repetitive tones indicating an outgoing connection.
"What damn time is it?" The agitated voice boomed through the speaker, an indignant protest at an unwelcome intrusion. "Zek? What... It's not even six a.m...."
Zecsa, wasting no time on pleasantries, interjects. Breaking the asymmetrical conversation with a surge of urgency and conviction in her voice. "I know, I know, but Gil, I think I've got an opportunity, a real job," she declares, her words infused with unwavering assurance.
The brief pause that followed was punctuated by muffled sounds of movement; the restless stirrings of a man torn away from sleep. Then, like a tempest, the male voice returns, laced with frustration. "At six in the morning?!" it erupted, reaching an almost shrill pitch.
Adopting a feigned air of apology, Zecsa continues, determined to convey the magnitude of her nocturnal discovery. "Alright, I’m sorry for waking you, but this is real man; I've dug up some deets, but I need some runners. And, if I recall correctly, you once mentioned that I should reach out to you... right?"
The speaker and microphone now seemed out of place on her cluttered desk, as if embodying the displaced voice of the unseen irate man, yearning to escape the tense conversation. Zecsa shook her head, gulping down a generous portion of the freshly brewed coffee in an attempt to fortify herself.
"Zek, I love you, babe, but can this wait? Call me later… or… whatever."
Frustration was etched on her face, a vivid portrait of someone desperate for sleep yet undeniably awake and annoyed. "Fine, whatever, man..." With a decisive motion, Zecsa slams her palm against the display, abruptly terminating the call.
"Okay, so not Gil. But then, who?"
Zecsa's gaze returned to the directory displayed before her, her fingers scrolling through the list. Some names elicited a wince, while others prompted a momentary pause, as she pondered their existence, or lack thereof, people in this business had a tendency to feature in the obituaries section.
The scrolling continued, a seemingly endless loop of dismissal until her eyes were fixated on a particular name. Her hand intending to press the icon to start a new call, lingered in suspended animation, frozen by a hesitancy that shot ripples of regret from her fingertips to her brain.
"It would be stupid to reach out to 'him' again," her inner voice reasoned, a reluctant weight of knowing in the concept of ‘him’; attempting to impose logic on her otherwise impulsive actions.
Her internal objection is acknowledged but overridden, a momentary eternity later, Zecsa's finger finally presses the icon. The familiar, melodic connection bleeps ripple through the air, a comforting lullaby to her weary mind.
A deep, heavily accented male voice broke the impromptu serenity. "Well, looka this... Zexsa," it drawled, leaving an unheard low laugh behind it, a mix of surprise and intrigue in the words.
Zecsa sits up straight in her chair, an unconscious response; fleetingly she glances at the blinking icon for including video in the call— her skin crawls as, with relief, she acknowledges its inactivity.
She doesn’t want to see this person, and is thankful she cannot be seen. The involuntary pause of the moment snaps her focus again.
Flatly, dispassionately, she replies;
"Hi, Davie."
Chapter Three:	Making Links
A slow flashing red light, Lynx was used to system failures, just a blip in his street life. He sat perched on a graffiti-strewn corner of the neon-lit metropolis; his eyes fixated on the holographic display of his deck.  The compact marvel he held was every technophile thief's favourite tool, a blend of custom hardware and AI programs for effortlessly breaching systems. To Lynx, it was more than a tool—it was a gateway to liberation from societal norms.
Mastering the deck came naturally to Lynx, like breathing. While other teenagers worried about exams or sought acceptance in corporate induction programs, those things held no power over him. His life was complex and difficult, but it was part of the game. He had occasionally skirted perilously close to the clutches of Cit-Sec, or corpo net security, but always stayed a step ahead. In the digital realm, he was an enigmatic phantom, where ordinary identities could be exposed or expunged in an instant.
The crimson warning glaring on the screen served only to irritate him. In an act of puerile defiance, Lynx deftly reprogrammed one of the internal AI systems, turning the nuisance into a dazzling digital fireworks display. "Stupid system, stupid blockers..." he muttered under his breath, his aggravation joining the pang of hunger that haunted him.
Breaking his attention away from the deck's lights, as though roused from a daydream, Lynx looked about in sudden realization, apparently having momentarily forgotten his surroundings.
The bustling metropolis wrapped about him, filled with the deep myriad of individuals forming the machinery of urban existence. Disapproving gazes met his unconventional presence, but their judgment meant nothing to him. Gathering his hardlines, he hurriedly stuffed them into his pack without a second thought. The deck, now adorned with mesmerizing visuals, joined the tangle of wire. Lynx smirked, pleased with the display, as he closed the pack.
The day appeared pleasant enough, but hunger shadowed Lynx. He yearned for nourishment, a very real link to the physical world that he had sorely neglected. Unfortunately, his usual sanctuary—a noodle stall operated by a friendly old guy— was cordoned off by Cit-Sec. This formidable police force, instead of safeguarding the public's interests, merely reinforced the divisive power structure of the city, maintaining a twisted hierarchy of "them" versus "us."
The cordon, a mere placative façade, masking Akururei’s truth. Just beyond the privacy barriers, Underappreciated and underpaid chooms in hazmat gear cleansed the area. The pavement, painted with blood— another nameless mass of flesh chewed up and spat out by the city.
With the noodle guy absent, hope for a free meal had evaporated. Lynx, with his stomach rumbling in protest, slowed his pace momentarily, craning his neck, attempting to glimpse behind the cordoned-off area. A robotic Cit-Sec sentinel stationed nearby seemed to fix its unwavering metallic gaze on him, a chilling reminder that his presence may attract unwelcome scrutiny. Hastening his steps, he reluctantly abandoned his curiosity, acknowledging that now was not an opportune moment to test the limits of his luck.
 
The dilapidation of the streets became ever more apparent, shuttered shop fronts daubed with florescent glowing paints, the common traffic of the hub or the Skrim gave way to an occasional car or transport wagon, the crowds thinned until it was only an occasional face; a homeless guy, some street-tough with implanted spikes instead of hair; The Skrim was vast and it’s southern-most area’s were part housing part industrial, it smelled of rust.
The day crawled by, each passing moment stretching with a weight that mirrored Lynx's growing desperation. So far, his efforts had yielded little more than a stolen can of drink— a direct act of larceny rather than his usual intricate digital deceptions.
Lynx knew these streets, he’d walked from south-central by the express road that dissected the city and made his way south through the Skrim, nearly as far as the docks. Yet, as the sun sank lower on the horizon, daylight being replaced by the vibrant glare of the few ever-open shops that braved the dark hours and blazing unending adverts, selling everything to nobody, he faced the stark reality of another night spent on the unforgiving streets; unless he could conjure something extraordinary.
Lynx scoured the darkening streets for a glimmer of respite. His weary eyes darted from one dilapidated building to another, searching for an empty apartment window or any semblance of shelter. With each passing moment, and as the lights of the more populace areas had given way to the grim gloom of the less inhabited more concreted cubes of industry, Lynx’s uncertainty and resignation grew. Finally, his gaze settled upon a box-motel nestled deep within a shadowy back alley— an abode where no sane person would willingly tread, its humming neon sign a flickering welcome in the dusk.
Approaching the building, Lynx's eyes zeroed in on the telltale signs of connectivity. A liminal grey box protruding from a nondescript rear wall, an unnoticed participant in the bigger world. Without hesitation, he dropped his worn backpack to the ground, swiftly retrieving his deck. The familiar weight of the device in his hands filled him with a sense of purpose.
With deft movements, he skillfully connected wires to the net-hub, a tangible link to the ethereal. His fingertips danced across the controls of the deck, a symbiotic dance between man and machine. The screens flickered to life, and the vibrant output lights illuminated the darkened alley as if heralding a small victory.
